# AEV-PLIG soft negatives A (shape twins) and C (in-place edits): result

Date 2026-11-05. Tree `/scratch/u6xq/hussain.u6xq/aevplig-paired` (mirror `~/isambard-ssh/aevplig-paired`). Plan `docs/plans/2026-11-05-aevplig-soft-negatives-AC.md`; design `docs/zernike_soft_negatives_design.md`; tables `work/output/eval/REPORT_softneg_arms.md`.

## Verdict

**Null for affinity, with a clean mechanism check.** Both schemes are learned by the structure model (twin rank accuracy 0.48 → 0.83, contact-edit rank accuracy 0.43 → 0.99), construction A is unsolvable by ligand identity (the ligand-only model trained with it collapses), and neither scheme nor their combination moves FEP-benchmark ranking, CASF or held-out-series ranking beyond seed noise. On a target-group-disjoint holdout both schemes make pointwise prediction worse (Pearson −0.05 to −0.07, RMSE +0.19 to +0.29 per seed, consistent across seeds). This closes the paired-data line for AEV-PLIG: three constructions (within-series ranking, pose decoys, descriptor-selected soft negatives) all leave FEP ranking where the vanilla recipe puts it.

## Setup

- Data: PDBbind + BindingNet + BindingDB-DCS (ligsim90) with (i) the 176 held-out series of arm 1 and (ii) a new target-group-disjoint holdout: 55 of 1,091 eligible target groups (UniProt merged over non-generic PDBbind names) drawn at random, all their rows from every source moved to `group_valid` (5,124 rows); train = 86,965 crystal/modelled rows. Baseline `base-g` retrained on this set.
- A (kinds 3/4): per PDBbind anchor, up to 2 twins = ligands from other target groups in the nearest 1% by occupancy-Zernike shape, lowest pocket complementarity in the property channels, marginal filters (|ΔlogP| ≤ 1.5, same charge sign, |Δaromatic| ≤ 1, size ±20%), excluded if sharing lig_key/group/template with the anchor or being a known binder of the group; placed by 10 ETKDG conformers + Crippen-O3A overlay on the crystal ligand + rigid soft-repulsion relief, acceptance at 3.0 Å; the cognate ligand regenerated by the identical path is the positive (kind 3). 31,730 twins / 14,481 positives on train anchors; after the twin rule (twin ligand must be a kind-0 train row) and orphan drops: 25,148 / 13,377. Cue AUROC kind 3 vs 4 on min_dist 0.51, n_within_3A 0.44, heavy atoms 0.49, logP 0.50.
- C (kinds 5/6/7): six MMP-style edits at the crystal pose (COOH→Me, NH2→Me, phenyl→cyclohexyl (contact only with an aromatic partner), OH→F, aromatic NH→O, amide NH→NMe), coordinate-mapped embedding + restrained MMFF + relief; contact site = complementary protein partner within rule radius (kind 6, hinge), exposed = no protein heavy atom within 3.5 Å (kind 7, label kept); null edit through the same path = kind 5. Train rows: 14,168 / 19,955 / 9,703. Cue AUROCs 5 vs 6: 0.58 / 0.43 / 0.47; 5 vs 7: 0.51 / 0.48 / 0.32.
- Loss: MSE on kinds {0,3,5,7} + 1.0 × hinge below the parent's label (margin 1.0 pK) on kinds {4,6}. Same sampler/seeds/checkpoint rule as before; 3 seeds; ligand-only gates for base-g, A, C.

## Reading

1. **The constructions worked as constructions.** The model learned to put twins 3.8 pK and contact edits 5.7 pK below the positives, with contact-distance cues at chance. A's negatives cannot be solved from the ligand alone: the ligand-only model trained with them loses 0.13 wPCC on FEP and 0.16 Pearson on CASF, because the same ligand is a positive in another pocket. That is the matching rule doing its job.
2. **C leaks an "edited molecule" cue.** The ligand-only C model still separates contact edits (rank acc 0.97, gap 2.2 pK) and pushes exposed edits down by 0.9 pK: an edited chemotype is recognisable without the protein, and the exposed-site controls (9.7k vs 20k contact edits, and no buffer between the 3.5 Å exposed rule and the 4–4.5 Å contact rules) only partly counter it. The structure model shows the same leak at a third of the size (exposed spread 0.33 → 0.53 pK).
3. **No affinity gain anywhere; a consistent loss on unseen targets.** Every FEP, CASF and series metric is within seed noise of base-g or below it; the group-disjoint pointwise Pearson drops by 0.05–0.07 at every seed for A, C and AC. Training the model to push plausible non-binders down makes it worse at absolute prediction on targets it has not seen.
4. **The automated shortcut gate must not be read literally here.** `compare_arms` reports "passes" for A because the ligand-only arm fell by more than the structure arm; the formula assumed the ligand-only arm measures a shortcut gain, not a collapse. The structure arm's own change is ≤ 0.
5. **FEP-benchmark resolution.** `base-g` differs from arm 1's `base` by −0.038 wPCC with no FEP target affected by the holdout (no series lost same-UniProt train rows); per-series Pearson swings up to ±0.5 between two 3-seed ensembles on near-identical data. Any effect smaller than ~0.04 wPCC needs far more than 3 seeds; none of the arms in this programme (within-series Δ, pose decoys, A, C, AC) came close.

## What stands at the end of the line

- Vanilla AEV-PLIG recipe unchanged; reproduces the paper (wPCC 0.590 / Kendall 0.428 at 10 members).
- Infrastructure that outlives the negatives: the group-disjoint split (`pocket_groups.py` + `--holdout-groups`), the pose-sensitivity and augmentation evaluators, the 15-PGDH external test (exact reproduction of the paper's predictions), Zernike pocket/ligand descriptors for 96,947 complexes (target recall@1 0.75; not a ligand proxy), and the generators themselves.
- Open: the 15-PGDH gap to Vina and FEP+ and the ligand-only parity on held-out series point to generalisation and pose/data quality (Hsu et al. 2025) rather than to the loss; the descriptors give a per-target "distance to training" axis for reporting that.
